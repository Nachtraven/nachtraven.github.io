A strong analytical question
    speaks to a genuine dilemma presented by your sources
    yields an answer that is not obvious
    suggests an answer complex enough

pt of tension -> analytical question -> thesis
Good analytical questions: 
    can highlight patterns/connections, or contradictions/dilemmas/problems.
    establish the scope of an argument, allowing you to focus on a manageable part of a broad topic or a collection of sources.
    address implications or consequences of your analysis

A thesis will generally respond to an analytical question or pose a solution to a problem that you have framed
When you frame you say what is at stake in your argument: why your question and the answer matter.
analytical claims: interpretation of facts, data, or sources --- vs --- normative claims: how things should be rather than how they are
