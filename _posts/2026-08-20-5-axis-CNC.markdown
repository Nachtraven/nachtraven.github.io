I have loved the idea of at home CNC machining for a long time. In the past, the niche of 2000-5000 eur machines was the realm of the motivated DIYer, retrofitting a manual mill or perhaps buying an import portal mill out of aluminium. Similarly to 3D printers, very little effort and solutions existed for user friendlyness and integration: no probing, ATCs, tool length detectors, cooling or air burst, or even enclosures.
Some tried to fix pieces of this: the [DMC2](https://shariffdmc.com/product/dmc2-cnc/) and Millenium Machines Milo/[Ratrig mill](https://ratrig.com/products/rat-rig-mill) came and went, and although the DMC2 seems interesting, it remains a kit that is not as user friendly (or marketed as hard) as the trendsetter in the mini CNC category: Makera and their [Carvera air](https://eu.makera.com/products/carvera-air). I have gotten the opportunity to see these in person working, and was not particularly impressed. Maybe the pro is better?

These compact 3-axis, often with an optional 4-axis machines come in at an "affordable" price with existing software support. However I find the explosion of 5 axis mini machines fascinating, and I will compare those bellow - it is difficult to collate details on these because of their nature of using images (!!) in their product descriptions, and to simply not give certain pieces of information that need to be gleamed from watching videos.

Additionally, it appears the problem of CAM has been "solved" (in a way) by these 5 axis machine companies using "AI" CAM. Something I believe is very relevant, but I am dubious about where the CAM runs, and the need to upload my designs to the cloud.

## Reviews

No money was exchanged, I have not used these machines, nor am I connected in any way to the manufacturers. I am comparing specs here because I couldn't find them in text form online - feel free to reach out if I'm wrong, or missing details. *All these products have million(s) in funding,* and it is important to note that **a lot of reviewers get a kickback from purchases (I have seen 5% and sometimes more) meaning they have a very large incentive to push you towards buying. They'd stand to make €250+ per sale!**

## Important notes

- **XYZ travel does not necessarily equal usable 5-axis work volume.** Rotary fixtures, spindle clearance, cutting-tool length and automatic tool-changer placement can reduce the actual machining envelope. Some, such as the PocketNC, have through axis stock holding possibilities, enabling machining of longer parts.

- **Machine weight is closely linked to rigidity given the same construction.** All the 3 axis machines chose a fixed portal, and some of the 5 axis machines just add a double rotary stage. The portal construction, as opposed to the classic column mills seen in regular 3 axis machines, gives the highest stifness to weight ratio. 

- **I wouldn't trust any of these for heavy/hard metals - and don't think you can use these to make money.** Given that I have not used any of these, I can't say for sure, but I am nearly certain that you wouldn't aim to do much more than aluminium on a regular basis. Steels appear possible but seem very slow, and with the stickout lengths in the videos, I doubt very accurate.

Additionally, making money using these machines in general is not the goal. Prototyping and learning maybe, but serious buyers wouldn't want a poorly made 5 axis part. The 3 axis machines however appear capable of making useful parts repeatedly in aluminium and it appears that small batches are very feasible.
